Pane

Pane Tested experiment

The whitespace, which no mark before this one ever measured. Plumb drew the cards on axis and left the empty space a radial fan — a third of the frame layout’s hole sat somewhere other than where the layout put it. Here the whitespace is cut from the ground as a band that sweeps in once the space is its own, and the claim it gives up leaves the auction in the same frame, so nothing else changes size. It is not the smooth one: read Plumb beside it.

  • The sidebar’s whitespace becomes a true rectangle; the frame’s goes from 31% wrong to 2%
  • Still one kind of card — every card a single-seed bidder, no card ever a wall
  • Corner budget intact and slivers better than Plumb’s at every clock
  • And it hitches: 3 to 8 stepped frames where Plumb has none, worst on a fast display

Plumb

Plumb Tested experiment

Bellows, drawn on axis. The edge between two cells is always perpendicular to the line joining their seeds, so a weight can slide an edge but never turn it: every angle on the page is decided by where the seeds sit, and a seam is drawable plumb only where the two cells it parts have the same extent along it. The sidebar's old template did not, so it fanned. This one lays its cells on a grid and gives the leftovers back as whitespace.

  • Rectangles where a person expects rectangles, with no wall and no second kind of cell
  • The sidebar goes from no true rectangle at all to more than half of them
  • Bellows' smoothness kept intact — the skip does not come back
  • plumb.js measures angle weighted by edge length, which a corner count cannot

Bellows

Bellows Tested experiment

The overflow buffer with its switch removed. Murmur's margin snaps into existence when a change starts and out of it when the change ends, and those two frames are the skip. Here there is no ring and no reserve: the ground itself is sized to the page's total claim, so it grows out of nothing as the travellers ask for room and returns to nothing as they land. At rest the domain is the window exactly.

  • Every cell gets exactly its claim, at any rim depth — an identity, not a ledger
  • A settled cell is untouched by anyone else's journey
  • No ring sites, so no extra neighbours and no extra corners
  • Read against Brim, which deletes the buffer instead

Brim

Brim Tested experiment

Murmur with the overflow buffer deleted. Murmur skips twice per change — 30 to 177 px in the frame the margin opens, 31 to 185 px in the frame it shuts, against neighbours moving 1 to 5 px — and the switch is the skip. Brim removes the switch by removing the thing that switches: the page is the window, always, and a travelling cell's room can only come from its neighbours. It is the control that says whether the buffer was ever needed.

  • No margin, no reserve, no loan: one domain, never resized
  • Every cell still a bidder — no walls, no blend, no flubber
  • The skip gone by construction; the question is what it costs
  • Read against Bellows, which keeps the buffer and makes it continuous

Murmur

Murmur Tested experiment

One kind of body, not two. The reference keeps three states in a cell — a bidder, a rectangle cut out of the ground, and a half-plane blend between them — and the blend is where the rippling lives. Murmur deletes the blend: every cell is a bidder, so three to nine corners is the shape a cell HAS rather than a rule policed against it, and a rectangle is somewhere to travel to. Nobody is released, so everybody has a path to follow.

  • Flubber 7.5% → 0.1%; the worst cell falls from 38 corners to 10
  • Cells fatter and less splintered than the reference’s at every clock
  • The margin is used by 5.6 cells at once over three edges, and is empty at rest
  • Steadier than the reference on its own clock: no cell jumps into place

Tide

Tide Tested experiment

The middle, rather than the two extremes. A rectangle is a DESTINATION, not a category: a template names the few slots that genuinely want to be cards and they land exact rectangles, while everybody else travels and rests as a convex Voronoi cell of three to nine corners. The reference could not express that — a scene gave every body a rectangle or none — so a page was either a grid or a flock.

  • Organic cells 17.7% → 62.2% of a change; flubber 7.5% → 0.6%
  • Cells fatter than the reference's, not just more numerous
  • Bento untouched: every slot a rectangle, as a bento should be
  • The corner budget is a consequence of who is bidding, not a clamp on a cell

Bleed

Bleed Tested experiment

Every cell a rectangle or a Voronoi cell. A settled tile asked to move travels as it is — a rigid rectangle, a hole in the auction's ground — and everything else is a convex power cell, cut straight where it meets a tile or the page. The page is wider than the window: one lattice cell of margin on every side, owned by a reserve that takes whatever the cards leave, and the settled page's edge cards run off all four sides of the crop.

  • No lattices of sites: nothing corrugates, nothing steps
  • Ink past the window in every frame of every change, cut by the crop, never bordered along it
  • Fewest teleports of any mark here; the last resort — a rectangle less a rectangle — named, counted and bounded
  • A margin view (the button, or ?view=bleed) draws the whole page

Tessera

Tessera Tested experiment

Rigid tiles on conforming lattices. A settled tile departs as it is, no pre-melt; along every seam a tile's edge column carries the rows of the column across it, so the seam is straight; whitespace is one bidder with a ring that conforms to every tile's edge; a packing pass gives travellers right of way.

  • No pre-melt: a settled tile moves as it is
  • Conforming seams: no two lattices corrugate against each other
  • Footprints yield and return; measured against Hive and Astra I

Hive · Spare

mk14 Live preview

Everything below, plus a hovered cell stops annihilating its neighbours. The ring a cell pushes out on hover was sized from its own rectangle alone: on the hero layout that is a quarter of the whole page, and the cards along the top — 116 px tall — were left as eleven-pixel bars holding two per cent of themselves. Then the page changes, the ring lets go, and all of it slides back in ten frames. Now the ring stops at a third of the thinnest neighbour it reaches. A cell with room around it grows exactly as before, to the pixel.

  • The top row stays readable while the hero is held
  • Jumps outside the cold settle: 21 to 8 at 30 ms, 11 to 7 at 60 fps
  • Bit-identical wherever nothing was being crushed

Hive · Masked

mk13 Live preview

Everything below, plus a cell holds its own contents. The number and the name follow their cell on a slower clock than the cell moves, and that lag is deliberate — it reads as weight. But they were drawn straight onto the canvas, so a label trailed outside the cell it belongs to and across whatever was behind it. Now the same path that paints the cell clips what it holds, so the lag shows as the content sliding under its own border.

  • A label never leaves its cell
  • The lazy follow is untouched
  • Paint only: the simulation is bit-identical to mk12

Hive · Guest

mk12 Live preview

Everything below, plus the pointer stops interfering. A cursor resting where it was is a moving cursor relative to a page rearranging under it: it catches every cell that flies past, and each catch is ground the auction hands out and takes straight back. So while a change runs the pointer may take no new cell, and the one it was holding is handed back across the change rather than dropped. Measured with a sweeping cursor, the jumping falls by 72%.

  • Jumps under a moving cursor: 116 to 32
  • Jumps with the cursor still, at 30 ms: 48 to 27
  • Hover is slower to arrive, on purpose — see the notes

Hive · Anticipation

mk11 Live preview

The clock round, plus the one anticipation that survived. Two bodies used to be told only how far apart they are right now, so neither yielded until both were already in the other's way and the push landed late and head-on. Now they are told how far apart they will be one spring's response time from now, so they swerve early and gently, and two already drawing apart are not pushed at all. Two other anticipations were built beside it and both failed honestly.

  • Cells read a neighbour's velocity and yield before contact
  • At rest it is the old law byte for byte, so the landed picture is whole
  • Costs one square root per pair

Hive · Clocks

mk10 Live preview

Every clock in the file measured in seconds. The cells fought and every measurement said they had stopped, because the whole harness ran at a flawless 60 fps and a browser gives whatever frame times it likes. Asked at 30 ms a frame, the same file scored four times the jumps. Three clocks were counted in frames: the auction's solving budget, a closing cell's catch-up, and the push that keeps a seed out of a wall. Now none of them are.

  • Jumps at 30 ms frames: 61 to 48
  • Auction error at 30 ms: 0.32% to 0.002%
  • Uncovered ground: 20 px² to 4
  • At 60 fps, exactly the identity

Hive

mk9 Kernel

The kernel, built from SEED.md. Agents own position and an area claim; a power-diagram auction honors every claim exactly, every frame. One verb — an agent changes its claim — covers layout, hover, insertion, removal. Exact rectangles at rest, organic in motion; a cell that holds cells is the same hive one level down. A scene change percolates: it reaches the cells through their shared edges, and each melts, travels and locks on its own clock. A settled cell leaves the auction and becomes a wall, so a moving cell has as many sides as it has neighbours; it becomes its rectangle by degrees, the way it became a cell. A hovered card grows the way a cell does, and the garment never draws a corner it cannot round.

  • Watertight by theorem, not by hope
  • Claims honored to 1e-6 (meters on-screen)
  • Lattice rest state: rigid grid, melts to travel
  • Fields: rigid outside, Voronoi inside, recursively
  • Pressure: a hovered cell bites through its border

Voroboids III

mk7 Prototype

Edge-first architecture. Voronoi scaffolding provides exact edges and vertices. Cells blend between scaffolding (when packed) and their own default shape (when separated). Edges disengage smoothly.

  • Edges are primary objects
  • Per-edge engagement blending
  • Max reach constraint (no starburst)
  • Builds on mk6 edge-first pipeline

Voroboids II

mk6 Prototype

Dual northstar system. Bisector-intersection targets pull cells toward where corners should meet. Corner-clustering northstars detect where they are meeting and snap them. Force + precision close the tessellation.

  • Triple-target attractors (the force)
  • Corner-cluster northstars (the snap)
  • Confidence-weighted alignment
  • Builds on mk5 atomic architecture

Voroboids

mk5 Prototype

Atomic cells with local contact. Each cell is a sovereign body with its own shape. Pairwise bisector clipping produces shared edges only where cells touch. Cells separate freely. No global tessellation, no shared mesh.

  • Cells are independent atoms
  • Contact is local and transient
  • Gaps are real empty space
  • Add/remove cells dynamically

Mesh Morph

mk4 Prototype

Half-edge mesh with fixed topology. Cells are persistent identities; layouts are vertex positions; transitions interpolate vertices. Per-cell rest areas with bounded elasticity — water balloons, not over-hydrated pizza.

  • Topology fixed at canonical mesh time
  • No Voronoi at runtime
  • Per-cell comfort ranges (upper + lower bounds)
  • Pops impossible by construction

Morph

mk2 Prototype

Universal cell primitive. Cells morph between any target layout — bento, hex, tetris, diagonal, spiral — with Voronoi guaranteeing valid tiling mid-flight.

  • Multiple target layouts as seed configs
  • Voronoi tiling valid at every frame
  • Collision-avoiding seed transitions
  • The travel is the hard problem
